### 0.2 Defaults I'll take unless you object

- **`canon import content-ts <path>`** reads `content.ts` **statically** with the TypeScript compiler API, through a small Node script that executes nothing. Go does the mapping and validation, as planned in M0–M1:
  - Each entry becomes an item: `project`, or `experience` for places inside sites.
  - `surfaces` and the `*Override`, `desktopOnly` and `portfolioOnly` fields become placements and per-surface copy.
  - `hide: true` becomes a draft, and `// TODO` and `// Private` comments become `private.notes`.
  - `videogamequest.me` URLs are rewritten to `rpg-quests.netlify.app`, and other non-owned links are flagged.
  - About, Contact and Settings are skipped (app chrome, not content).
  - `contentfulDescription` components become `richDescription.component` keys. The components stay in Nickel Tools.
  - Thumbnails from `nickeltools.dev/project-screenshots/...` are downloaded and ingested as `thumbnail` assets, sanitized like everything else.
  - It's rerunnable like the bucket import (items match by id and alias), and it writes a report.
- **The showroom's extras** (`LAB_EXTRAS`, "Curie x3" and the recipe API) are entered by hand as items with only a `holodeck-showroom` placement.
- **What each site PR changes:**
  - Loading the view (via J2) replaces the old source.
  - The site renders the same as before. Any visible difference is listed in the PR, such as the art room's image sizes now coming from Canon instead of Netlify's CDN.
  - The PR is tested against the live public view.
- **Visibility:** everything imported from `content.ts` that's visible today becomes `public` only after you've seen `canon view --drafts` for each surface. That review step happens per site, as with J1.
- **The old bucket:** Canon never writes to `artandmedia` (D7). Once both sites stop reading it, retiring it is your call. The copies stay.
